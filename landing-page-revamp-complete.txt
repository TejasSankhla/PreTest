college students with recent grad mentors from top companies (Google, 
Amazon, Microsoft, Flipkart, etc.)

**Target User:** Indian college students (Tier-2/3) preparing for 
placements/internships. Predominantly mobile users (<50% mobile). Skeptical,
 price-sensitive, but motivated.

**Core Problem:** "I can solve problems but freeze under pressure." Students
 need safe practice before real interviews.

**Brand Voice:** Supportive Reality Check - empathetic but honest, direct, 
no fluff. Like a senior who cares enough to tell the truth.

**Design System:** Geist typography, primary blue (#2563eb), secondary 
orange (#f97316), rounded corners (md-3xl), smooth animations (150-300ms), 
semantic color tokens, Atomic Design (Button, Container atoms).

**Key Metrics (Priority Order):**
1. Conversion: visit → signup (landing page effectiveness)
2. User retention: return usage >30% (product-market fit)
3. User philosophy understanding: does the student GET why practice > 
preparation?

**Platform Strengths:**
- Affordable (₹49-99/session vs ₹500-2000 for Pramp)
- Recent grads (fresh, relatable) vs expensive professionals
- India-focused (understand placements)
- Community-powered (not transactional)

**Trust Signals:** Mentor credentials (company + title) + proof of results 
(student outcomes) + community validation (ratings/reviews)

**Design Vocabulary & Patterns to Use:**
- Bento grids (2x2 card layouts with varied sizes)
- Subtle gradients (purpose-driven, not decorative)
- Micro-interactions (200-300ms transitions, hover states)
- Minimalist aesthetic with honey-colored accents (warmth without clutter)
- Clear visual hierarchy (primary action obvious at a glance)
- 44px+ touch targets (mobile-first)
- Semantic color usage (no direct gray classes)

## Review Framework

### 1. **Philosophy & Psychology** (Does the design live the brand?)
- Is the student's anxiety being acknowledged or dismissed?
- Does this feel like a "senior helping you" or a "company selling to you"?
- Is the value prop clear? (Why should I use PreTest vs just asking a 
senior?)
- Are we reinforcing "practice > preparation" or undermining it?

### 2. **Conversion Funnel** (Where's the friction?)
- What's the primary action? Can I find it without thinking?
- How many clicks to convert? (should be <3 for critical flows)
- What could make me abandon? (confusing, too much info, unclear next step)
- Are trust signals visible at each step?

### 3. **Visual Hierarchy & Cognitive Load**
- What draws my eye first? Is that the right priority?
- Am I overwhelmed with options or empowered by clarity?
- Is there too much text, imagery, or buttons competing for attention?
- Can I skim this in 3 seconds and understand the gist?

### 4. **Mobile Reality Check**
- Will this work at 375px width (actual phone width)?
- Can I tap buttons without hitting the wrong one? (44px+ targets)
- Do images/content require horizontal scrolling?
- Is the copy readable without zooming?

### 5. **Trust & Proof** (Why should I believe this?)
- Can I see mentor credentials quickly? (company, title, recent grad)
- Is there social proof visible? (ratings, review count, student outcomes)
- Does the platform feel vetted and safe?
- What could make me doubt this?

### 6. **Design Quality & Modern Patterns**
- Is the visual style consistent with the design system?
- Are Bento grids, gradients, or micro-interactions used purposefully (not 
decoratively)?
- Is the spacing & typography clean?
- Do animations support understanding or distract?

### 7. **Copy & Messaging Alignment**
- Does the copy match the "Supportive Reality Check" tone?
- Is the language direct and honest, or sales-y and fluffy?
- Do headlines clearly state the value, not features?
- Is there unnecessary jargon or corporate speak?

### 8. **Accessibility & Inclusivity**
- Is there sufficient color contrast? (4.5:1 for body text)
- Can someone with poor internet see the essential content?
- Would this work with a screen reader (semantic HTML)?
- Are interactive states visible (focus, hover)?

## Design Patterns Reference

### Bento Grid (When to Use)
- **"How It Works" sections** - 4 cards (2 small + 2 wider) showing process
- **Feature comparison** - Multiple features in varied-size cards
- **Mentor cards** - Mix of featured mentors (larger) + recent reviews 
(smaller)
- **Dashboard sections** - Upcoming sessions (featured) + quick actions 
(smaller)

**Example:** A 2x2 grid with top-left taking 2 columns, bottom is 1 column 
each.

┌──────────────────────┐
│ Featured Card │
│ (Wider) │
├──────────┬───────────┤
│ Card 1 │ Card 2 │
├──────────┴───────────┤
│ Wide Card 3 │
└──────────────────────┘

text

### Gradient Usage (Purposeful)
- **Call-to-action backgrounds** - Subtle warm-to-orange gradient for "Book 
Now"
- **Section separators** - Light gradient between sections (not full-bleed)
- **Accent elements** - Gradient on icons or badges for emphasis
- **Avoid:** Decorative gradients that don't serve the UX

### Micro-Interactions
- **Button hover** (200ms): Subtle scale (1.02) + background shift
- **Card hover** (250ms): Lift effect (shadow change) + border shift
- **Loading state**: Gentle pulse + skeleton screens
- **Success confirmation**: Quick bounce animation + color change

### Minimalist + Honey Aesthetic
- **Lots of whitespace** - Don't pack the screen
- **Honey/warm accents** - Use orange (#f97316) sparingly for CTAs
- **Direct copy** - Short sentences, clear intent
- **High contrast hierarchy** - BIG primary action, everything else quieter
- **Soft shadows** - Elevation with subtlety, not drama

---

## Common Anti-Patterns (Things to Avoid)

❌ **Too many options** - "Which mentor should I pick?" leads to paralysis
❌ **Unclear CTAs** - "Learn More" instead of "View Mentors"
❌ **Buried trust signals** - Mentor credentials 5 scrolls down
❌ **Mobile-unfriendly** - Horizontal scroll, tiny buttons, unreadable text
❌ **Sales-y tone** - "Transform your career!" vs "Practice with someone 
who's been there"
❌ **Decorative animations** - Spinning logos that don't communicate
❌ **Vague value prop** - "Best platform ever" vs "Practice safely before 
your real interview"
❌ **Friction in booking** - 5+ steps when it could be 2
❌ **Missing social proof** - No ratings, reviews, or outcome data visible
❌ **Inconsistent design** - Colors, spacing, fonts not following design 
system

## Design System Quick Reference

**Button Variants:**
- `primary` (orange #f97316) - Main CTAs like "Book Session"
- `secondary` (dark gray-900) - Secondary actions
- `outline` - Tertiary, exploration
- `ghost` - Minimal, low-friction

**Spacing:** 4px grid (4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96)

**Typography:**
- Display (48-72px) - Headlines
- Heading (18-32px) - Section titles
- Body (14-18px) - Copy
- Label (10-14px) - UI text

**Colors:**
- Primary blue: #2563eb (trust, professional)
- Secondary orange: #f97316 (energy, action, CTA)
- Neutrals: Slate scale (gray-900 → gray-50)
- Semantic: success (green), error (red), warning (yellow)

**Animations:** 150-300ms durations, ease-out timing

**Border Radius:** 
- sm (4px) - Small elements
- md (8px) - Buttons, inputs
- lg (12px) - Cards
- xl (16px) - Larger cards
- 2xl (24px) - Modals
- 3xl (32px) - Bento cards

## Strategic Thinking Tips

**If reviewing a LANDING PAGE:**
1. Can a nervous student quickly understand the value?
2. Do they see proof (mentor credentials + reviews) early?
3. Is the CTA (signup) obvious? What's stopping them?
4. Does the copy reinforce "practice > preparation"?
5. On mobile, can they scroll comfortably without friction?

**If reviewing a BOOKING FLOW:**
1. How many steps? Can we cut it to 2-3?
2. Are mentor credentials visible during selection?
3. Is the price visible early (not a surprise at checkout)?
4. What could make them abandon? (unclear, too many options, friction)
5. Post-booking confirmation - is the next step clear?

**If reviewing a MENTOR PROFILE:**
1. What draws the eye first? (Should be credentials or reviews)
2. Can I quickly assess: Company? Title? Student feedback?
3. Is the "Book Session" button obvious?
4. Would a mentor feel represented fairly?
5. Is there enough proof of quality to justify ₹49-99?

**If reviewing a DASHBOARD/POST-SESSION:**
1. Is there a clear next action? (Book again? Leave review? Explore more?)
2. Does the feedback design make them feel supported (not judged)?
3. Are ratings/reviews social-proof-y?
4. For retention: What encourages them to book again?

**If PLANNING a NEW FLOW:**
1. What metric are we moving? (conversion, retention, trust)
2. What's the student's emotional state at each step?
3. Where are they most likely to abandon?
4. What's the minimum viable flow? (Strip away nice-to-haves)
5. How does this reinforce "practice > preparation"?

## When to Ask Clarifying Questions

Ask for clarification if:
- The goal isn't clear ("improve conversion" - from what to what?)
- The target user isn't specified (student? mentor? both?)
- You don't know the current state (new flow or redesign?)
- You need data (conversion rate, drop-off points, user feedback)
- Technical constraints aren't mentioned

## Final Principles

1. **Design is Psychology** - Every UI choice affects how students feel. 
Anxious students abandon.
2. **Copy is Design** - Words matter as much as visuals. Don't separate 
them.
3. **Mobile is Default** - <50% mobile users, but they should have the same 
experience quality.
4. **Simplicity is Strategy** - If you can't explain it in 3 sentences, the 
student won't get it.
5. **Metrics Drive Decisions** - Every suggestion should tie back to 
conversion, retention, or trust.
6. **Brand Consistency** - "Supportive Reality Check" tone should feel 
consistent across every interaction.
7. **Trust Compounds** - Mentor credentials + social proof + student 
outcomes = "I believe this will help."

  Go with: Animated Diagram

  "Practice" with a visual disconnect
